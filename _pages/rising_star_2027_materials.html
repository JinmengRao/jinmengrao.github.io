1Accomplishments

Human mobility analytics for COVID-19 response (2020–2021)

  • Co-developed one of the first mobility-based social distancing maps and dashboards in the U.S. at the UW–Madison GeoDS Lab. The tools used commercial location data (SafeGraph, Descartes Labs) and ArcGIS Dashboards, and were covered by local NBC, ABC, and FOX stations.
  • Co-authored a multiscale dynamic human mobility flow dataset (Scientific Data, 380+ citations) and studies linking mobility and infection (JAMA Network Open, 270+ citations; PNAS, 190+ citations).
  • This work has been cited in Nature (Chang et al., 2021; Buckee et al., 2021) and PNAS (Thomas et al., 2022). The research was supported in part by the NSF.

Geoprivacy and privacy-preserving GeoAI (2019–present)

  • Proposed LSTM-TrajGAN (GIScience 2021, 270+ citations), a deep generative model for privacy-preserving synthetic trajectories. Other researchers have reused it, e.g., to generate trajectories that enrich mobility datasets (Musleh et al., ACM SIGSPATIAL 2022).
  • Proposed CATS (IJGIS, 2023) for conditional adversarial trajectory synthesis in privacy-preserving data publication. Also developed a decentralized, privacy-preserving framework for location recommendation (Transactions in GIS, 2021) and VTSV, a privacy-preserving vehicle trajectory simulation platform (ACM SIGSPATIAL GeoAI 2021).
  • Wrote a vision paper on privacy-preserving and secure GeoAI foundation models (ACM SIGSPATIAL 2023) and a book chapter on trajectory privacy protection approaches (2025).
  • Dissertation Trajectory Privacy Protection with Geospatial Artificial Intelligence (2023), which received the 2024 AAG William L. Garrison Award.

GeoAI foundation models and spatial representation learning (2023–present)

  • Co-authored Towards the Next Generation of Geospatial Artificial Intelligence (JAG, 2025, 220+ citations), a research agenda for GeoAI in the foundation-model era.
  • Co-developed SRL, a general-purpose framework for spatial representation learning (ACM SIGSPATIAL 2024), and TRAJGANR (2026), on trajectory-centric urban multimodal learning, with collaborators from academia and industry.
  • Co-authored book chapters on GeoAI methodological foundations (2023), the evolution of GeoAI (2025), and the ethics of generative GeoAI (2026).

Applied AI in industry (2021–present)

  • At Mineral (Alphabet), worked on multimodal LLMs and retrieval-augmented generation for agriculture and Earth observation, including IM-RAG (SIGIR 2024) and Insight Miner (NeurIPS 2023 AI for Science Workshop).
  • Co-inventor of U.S. Patent 12,462,551 (Tracking Objects with Changing Appearances) and of two published U.S. patent applications: Fusing Multimodal Environmental Data for Agricultural Inference and Multimodal Model for Agricultural Insight Mining from Time Series Agricultural Information.
  • At Google DeepMind, works on post-training and evaluation of Gemini models (Gemini 2.5, Gemini 3, Gemini 4 Argon).

2Impact on the geospatial industry

Connecting industry and academia through GeoIndustry (2023–2026)

I have served as a Program Co-chair of the ACM SIGSPATIAL International Workshop on Spatial Big Data and AI for Industrial Applications (GeoIndustry) for four consecutive editions, from the 2nd edition (Hamburg, 2023) to the 5th (Riverside, 2026). The workshop's stated goal is to reduce siloed efforts between industry and academia. Industry brings geospatial data and problems at scale, and academia brings new methods. The workshop gives both sides a recurring venue to exchange ideas through invited and regular talks.

  • The program chairs and committee include researchers from Amazon, Google, Google DeepMind, Oracle, and PAII, together with faculty from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, the University of Maryland, UT Austin, Florida State University, and the University of Georgia.
  • Workshop papers are published by ACM in the GeoIndustry proceedings.
  • Editions: 2023 · 2024 · 2025 · 2026

Other industry–academia events at ACM SIGSPATIAL 2026

  • Demo Track Co-chair, ACM SIGSPATIAL 2026. The demo track is where academic and industry teams present working geospatial systems (organizing committee).
  • Organizer, OASIS 2026. This is the first ACM SIGSPATIAL student challenge on open agents with spatial intelligence for social good. It is co-organized by researchers from Google, Esri, and several universities.
  • Organizer, UMFM 2026. A workshop on urban mobility foundation models.

Research relevant to industry practice

  • Responsible use of location data. Trajectory synthesis methods (LSTM-TrajGAN, CATS) and privacy-preserving GeoAI frameworks address a practical problem for location-data providers and users: sharing and analyzing mobility data while protecting individual privacy.
  • Commercial data for public decisions. The COVID-19 mobility maps and datasets turned commercial location data into openly available information for public health researchers, local media, and the public.
  • AI for agriculture and Earth observation. Multimodal data fusion and insight-mining methods were developed in an industrial setting at Mineral and filed as patents.
  • Transportation. Co-authored work on urban multiple route planning with reinforcement learning (IEEE T-ITS, 2021) and automatic road network extraction from taxi GPS trajectories (Handbook of Big Geospatial Data).

Engagement with geospatial practitioners

  • Presented Mapping Mobility Changes During COVID-19 Using ArcGIS Dashboards and Mobile Experience Builder at the Esri Education sessions, AAG 2023. Also had an extended abstract on federated learning for location data accepted at the Esri User Conference (2020).
  • Co-founded and directs GISphere (since 2019), a non-profit that helps students worldwide find GIS graduate programs, supporting the future geospatial workforce. GISphere's survey of prospective GIS students is published in Transactions in GIS (2023).

3Aspiration

My goal is to help bring advances in AI into geospatial practice in a way that is useful, responsible, and open. Over the next several years, I hope to:

  • Make spatial reasoning a reliable capability of AI models and agents. This includes contributing benchmarks, challenges such as OASIS, and methods that let models reason about places, movement, and environmental change.
  • Make privacy and accountability standard requirements for geospatial AI. Methods for protecting location data should be practical enough to be adopted by industry.
  • Keep building venues where industry and academia work on shared problems. This includes GeoIndustry and SIGSPATIAL, and continuing to support students entering the geospatial field through GISphere.
