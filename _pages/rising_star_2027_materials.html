This page summarizes my work for the Geospatial World 50 Rising Stars 2027 nomination committee. My geospatial work centers on three areas: geoprivacy (methods that let location data be shared and analyzed while protecting individuals), public health (mobility analytics for the COVID-19 response), and community building (venues that connect geospatial industry, academia, and students). The page follows the three areas in the nomination form (accomplishments, impact on the geospatial industry, and aspiration), followed by recognition, service, and supporting links.

1Accomplishments

Geoprivacy: responsible use of location data (2019–present)

  • Proposed LSTM-TrajGAN (GIScience 2021, 270+ citations), a deep generative model for privacy-preserving synthetic trajectories, released as open-source code (80+ stars, 30+ forks).
  • Proposed CATS (IJGIS, 2023) for conditional adversarial trajectory synthesis in privacy-preserving data publication. Also developed a decentralized, privacy-preserving framework for location recommendation (Transactions in GIS, 2021) and VTSV, a privacy-preserving vehicle trajectory simulation platform (ACM SIGSPATIAL GeoAI 2021).
  • Wrote a vision paper on privacy-preserving and secure GeoAI foundation models (ACM SIGSPATIAL 2023) and a book chapter on trajectory privacy protection approaches (2025).
  • Dissertation Trajectory Privacy Protection with Geospatial Artificial Intelligence (2023), which received the 2024 AAG William L. Garrison Award (one recipient every two years).
  • Uptake: LSTM-TrajGAN has become a reference model for synthetic trajectory generation. Other groups have extended it (to enforce differential privacy, IEEE Access 2026; for selective partial anonymization, IEEE Access 2023; with Transformer decoders, MLNLP 2022), built it into processing pipelines (IEEE Access 2023; vehicle-trajectory protection, IEEE Internet of Things Journal 2024), and re-implemented it as the state-of-the-art benchmark (PETS 2022 and 2024; Information Systems Research 2023). Industry researchers at Lenovo (FGCS 2024), State Grid (IEEE T-ITS 2022), Rakuten (IEEE BigData 2022), and ByteDance (IJGIS 2025) have drawn on the trajectory privacy work. Details are in the uptake table.

Public health: mobility analytics for COVID-19 response (2020–2021)

  • Co-developed one of the first mobility-based social distancing maps and dashboards in the U.S. at the UW–Madison GeoDS Lab (SIGSPATIAL Special, 2020, 410+ citations). The tools used commercial location data (SafeGraph, Descartes Labs) and ArcGIS Dashboards, and were covered by local NBC, ABC, and FOX stations.
  • Co-authored a multiscale dynamic human mobility flow dataset (Scientific Data, 380+ citations) and studies linking mobility and infection (JAMA Network Open, 270+ citations; PNAS, 190+ citations).
  • This work has been cited in Nature (Chang et al., 2021; Buckee et al., 2021) and PNAS (Thomas et al., 2022). The research was supported in part by the NSF.
  • Uptake: the open flow dataset (GitHub, 150+ stars) became a widely used input for mobility and epidemic modeling. For example, the Deep Gravity mobility-flow model co-developed by Amazon researchers (Nature Communications, 2021) was evaluated on it. Industry research teams at Microsoft Research (Nature, 2021), Google (Nature Communications, 2021), Meta, Tencent, Orange, IQVIA, and DiDi (WWW 2026) drew on these results in their own mobility and epidemic studies.

2Impact on the geospatial industry

Why this work matters to the geospatial industry

  • Geoprivacy for the location-data industry. Companies that collect or analyze location data (mobile apps, mobility platforms, connected vehicles) need to share and analyze trajectories without exposing individuals. LSTM-TrajGAN and CATS generate synthetic trajectories that keep the useful patterns of real data while protecting privacy. Industry researchers have drawn on this work in vehicle-network privacy (State Grid, IEEE T-ITS 2022), trajectory privacy models (Lenovo, FGCS 2024), and trajectory-user linking (Rakuten, IEEE BigData 2022).
  • Commercial location data for public health. The COVID-19 maps and datasets turned commercial mobility data (SafeGraph, Descartes Labs) into open information through Esri ArcGIS Dashboards, used by the public, local media, and public health researchers. The open flow dataset became a benchmark for mobility models, including the Deep Gravity model co-developed by Amazon researchers, and the work is drawn on by researchers at Microsoft Research, Google, Meta, Tencent, Orange, IQVIA, and DiDi.
  • Community that links industry and academia. GeoIndustry, the SIGSPATIAL 2026 demo track, and the OASIS challenge (co-organized with Google and Esri researchers) give industry and academic teams shared venues, while GISphere helps students worldwide enter GIS graduate programs and the geospatial workforce.

Connecting industry and academia through GeoIndustry (2023–2026)

I have served as a Program Co-chair of the ACM SIGSPATIAL International Workshop on Spatial Big Data and AI for Industrial Applications (GeoIndustry) for four consecutive editions, from the 2nd edition (Hamburg, 2023) to the 5th (Riverside, 2026). The workshop's stated goal is to reduce siloed efforts between industry and academia. Industry brings geospatial data and problems at scale, and academia brings new methods. The workshop gives both sides a recurring venue to exchange ideas through invited and regular talks.

  • The program chairs and committee include researchers from Amazon, Google, Google DeepMind, Oracle, and PAII, together with faculty from the University of Wisconsin–Madison, the University of Maryland, UT Austin, Florida State University, and the University of Georgia.
  • Workshop papers are published by ACM in the GeoIndustry proceedings.
  • Editions: 2023 · 2024 · 2025 · 2026

3Aspiration

My goal is to help the geospatial industry use location data and AI in ways that are useful, responsible, and open. Over the next several years, I hope to:

  • Make privacy protection a standard part of geospatial AI. Methods for protecting location data should be practical enough for location-data providers and mobility platforms to adopt by default, and should extend to geospatial foundation models and AI agents.
  • Apply location data and spatial reasoning to public health and social good. This includes building benchmarks and challenges, such as OASIS, that test whether AI models and agents can reason reliably about places, movement, and environmental change.
  • Keep building the community. Continue GeoIndustry and SIGSPATIAL venues where industry and academia work on shared problems, and continue supporting students entering the geospatial field through GISphere.
